WebP vs PNG — Key Differences

WebP is a modern format developed by Google that supports both lossy and lossless compression, typically producing files 25-35% smaller than equivalent PNGs. PNG uses lossless DEFLATE compression and has been a web standard since the late 1990s, making it compatible with essentially all software. Both formats support full alpha transparency, but PNG enjoys universal support in image editors, email clients, and legacy systems where WebP may not be recognized. The trade-off is straightforward: WebP saves bandwidth while PNG guarantees compatibility.

When to Use PNG Instead of WebP

Choose PNG when you need to share images with people who may use older software, email clients, or platforms that do not yet support WebP. PNG is also the preferred format for graphic design workflows, print preparation, and any situation where you need guaranteed lossless quality with transparency in a universally accepted format.

Frequently Asked Questions

Will I lose transparency when converting WebP to PNG?

No. PNG fully supports alpha transparency, so any transparent areas in your WebP image will be preserved exactly in the PNG output.

Why is the PNG file larger than the WebP?

WebP uses more advanced compression algorithms than PNG. A lossless WebP file is typically 25-35% smaller than its PNG equivalent. The larger PNG size is the trade-off for universal format compatibility.

Can I convert animated WebP to PNG?

This tool converts the first frame of an animated WebP. For animated content, consider converting to GIF or keeping the WebP format.

Is my image uploaded to a server?

No. The conversion happens entirely in your browser using the HTML Canvas API. Your image never leaves your device.
