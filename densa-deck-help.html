Getting started

The phone app works entirely on its own. A PC is an addition, not a requirement.

This phone only, or with a PC Free

The first thing the app asks is whether you have a desktop. Choose This phone only and nothing ever asks again — no pairing, no network, no account. You can connect a PC later from Settings, and you can disconnect one the same way.

The card index Free

Before scanning works, the phone needs to know what cards exist. It downloads that once — about 79 MB from Scryfall, or faster from your PC if you have one paired. After that, scanning, searching and pricing all work with no signal at all.

Your collection lives on the phone Free

Everything you scan is stored in a single database on the device. Edits made with no signal are kept and exchanged with the desktop when it is next in reach; nothing is lost in the meantime and nothing is uploaded anywhere.

Scanning cards

The phone reads the card itself. The set code and collector number along the bottom edge are what identify it exactly.

Capture, or auto scan Free

Capture takes one picture. Auto scan keeps taking them while you feed cards past the camera, and stops on its own if the camera freezes or nothing is being read. The phone vibrates when a card is seen, not on every frame.

Add cards, or tag what you own Free

Add cards files each scan into the collection you pick. Tag what I own puts cards you already have into a group — a bundle to sell, a pile to lend — without adding or removing anything. Tagging needs a PC. Needs a PC

Foil, and prerelease promos Free

Two switches at the top of the scanner, because neither can be read off the card reliably. Foil stays on until you turn it off — foils come in runs — and the green confirmation says FOIL so a mistake is one card old rather than thirty. Prerelease promo files the date-stamped printing instead of the ordinary one.

How many, and which printing Free

When a card needs a choice, the picker shows the actual card art for each printing, with the set name, number and rarity colour. Set a quantity first to file several at once. For a card with hundreds of printings there is a set box to narrow it down.

Basic lands Free

An Island has 671 printings in the index, so the name alone cannot identify one. If the collector number will not read, the app falls back to the artist and copyright year along the bottom edge, which narrows most basics to a handful of cards you can pick by sight. Cards printed before 1995 carry no set code at all, and for those this is the only route.

Typing a card in Free

Under the camera there is a name box. Some cards the camera was never going to place — a worn land, a card in a heavy sleeve — and typing four letters is faster than fighting it.

Your collection

Collections and groups Free

A card is filed in one place — that is where the physical card lives — and can be tagged into any number of lists on top of that. Deleting a group needs a PC. Needs a PC

Browsing and searching Free

Search by name, rules text, colour, type, rarity, set or mana value. Only mine narrows it to what you own, and can be pointed at a single collection — “only the cards in my Modern binder”.

Wrong printing, or wrong finish Free

Open a card you own and you can move it onto a different printing, or mark it as foil after the fact — all of it, or just one copy of four. Nothing is deleted on the way: the new stack is created before the old one is reduced.

Card art Free

Every picture comes from Scryfall and is cached on the device after it has been seen once. Nothing is bundled into the app and nothing is re-hosted.

Building decks

Browse, or paste a list Free

Search the catalogue and tap to add, or paste a decklist from anywhere — set codes, collector numbers and Moxfield-style *F* foil markers are all understood and stripped off the card name. The text box is the deck; editing it and saving rewrites the list.

Tap to add Free

A checkbox under the filters. With it on, a tap adds the card immediately at its cheapest printing rather than opening it to be read. Hold a card to open it anyway — to read it, swipe the printings, or take one out.

Scanning straight into a deck Free

Open a deck and there is a scanner with the same camera, picker and switches. Two modes: From my collection puts the card in the deck and leaves your collection alone, and New card files it as well. If you scan something that is not catalogued, it says so and offers to file it — or to put it in the deck anyway, which is the right answer for a proxy or a borrowed card.

Commander, colours and deck art Free

Set a commander and the browser locks to its colour identity, greying anything outside it with a reason rather than hiding it. The deck shows its colours as pips with a count of each, so you can see a splash from an even pair. Any card in the deck can be chosen as the deck’s picture — it does not have to be the commander.

Foil slots Free

A deck slot records which finish is going in the sleeve, so your foil copy and your plain one are two different slots, counted and priced apart. The readable decklist stays clean — no *F* down the side — and the finish is restored when the text comes back.

What you still need Free

The deck is checked against what you own and tells you the shortfall, plus format warnings — size, copy limits, colour identity.

Prices and value

Where the numbers come from Free

Scryfall. Prices arrive with the card index and are refreshed for the cards you own and the cards your decks point at — a couple of requests rather than another download. There is a button in Settings, and it happens along with the card check when prices are more than a week old.

A foil is priced as a foil Free

Where the copy on your shelf is the shiny one, the deck is valued at the foil price. Where you own both and the slot does not say which, it takes the lower number: a total that guesses upward reads as a valuation and is a wish.

A card with no price is not free Free

Cards Scryfall has no price for are counted and reported separately — “3 cards couldn’t be priced” — rather than folded in as zero.

The card index

Keeping up with new sets Free

Settings can check whether a set has come out that the phone does not have. Checking is tiny; downloading is not, so it never downloads without asking. You can opt in to it checking for you — it follows Scryfall’s real release calendar, and looks at least once a quarter regardless.

Sets that will never arrive Free

The index keeps English paper cards. A few released sets have none — Foreign Black Border, Renaissance, Rinascimento and others are non-English printings of cards that exist in English elsewhere. After a complete download the app writes those off so it stops reporting them as missing.

What a PC adds

All of this is still free. The desktop app is a free download; a licence only matters for the Pro section below.

Deck analysis and suggestions Needs a PC

Mana curve, archetype detection, colour and type breakdowns, and recommendations for what to add or cut. The phone asks the desktop for these; it cannot compute them alone.

Combo detection and Rule 0 Needs a PC

Two-card and longer combos in your deck, near-misses you are one card away from, and a pre-game disclosure sheet naming the lines. Combo data is from Commander Spellbook.

Bracket fit Needs a PC

Where the deck sits on the 1–5 Commander bracket scale, and what to change if it is pitched wrong.

Building from your collection Needs a PC

Ask the desktop to assemble a deck out of what you actually own. The judging is over the whole catalogue, which is why it needs the machine with the whole catalogue on it.

Two devices, one collection Needs a PC

Edits travel both ways as events rather than as totals, so a card added on the phone with no signal and a card added on the PC at the same time both survive. Settings shows what each device thinks you own, which is the only place that can tell you they disagree.

Exports Needs a PC

Decklists out to MTGA, MTGO and Moxfield formats, and reports as JSON, Markdown or HTML.

Everything above is free

Densa Deck Pro

A one-time purchase, activated on the desktop. No subscription, no server, and it keeps working offline. The phone picks it up on the next sync.

See pricing on the product page

What Pro adds

All of it on the desktop. The phone shows the results when the desktop is in reach.

Goldfish simulation Pro

Play the deck out against no opposition, thousands of games, and see how fast it does what it is trying to do — including how often it assembles a combo and on which turn.

Matchup gauntlet Pro

The deck against eleven archetype profiles, with win rates and the reason each game ended.

Probability tools Pro

Opening-hand simulation, mana development, and the odds of having a given card by a given turn.

Deep analysis Pro

Synergy, castability, mana base quality and a 1–10 power level rating, plus exporting the full report.

Version history Pro

Save a deck as a version, compare any two, see what a change did to the deck’s power and whether it broke a combo.

The analyst and coach Pro

A local AI that has read your deck: explain why a card is in it, compare two decks in prose, and answer questions about the list. It runs on your machine — nothing is sent anywhere.

More saved decks and groups Pro

Free keeps a limited number of saved decks and groups of your own; Pro removes the limit. Scanning, filing and the size of your collection are unlimited either way. Settings on the phone shows your exact allowance.

What it will not do

Worth knowing before you go looking for them.

Guess at a card

The phone matches exactly or not at all. A card it cannot place is offered as a list to choose from, or refused with the reason — it never files a card it is not sure about. Fuzzy name matching lives on the desktop, where a person is looking at the screen.

Read a foil off the card

The foil marker in the collector line is the one glyph the recogniser reliably loses. That is why there is a switch rather than a guess.

Work out which copy is in a deck when you own both

If you own a foil and a plain copy of the same printing and the deck slot does not say which, the app cannot know. It takes the lower price rather than the flattering one. Set the finish on the slot to be exact.

Resume an interrupted index download

A bulk file has to be read from the start, so an interrupted download starts that file again. The printings are fetched first, so scanning works even if the second half is interrupted.

Record a card that does not exist

Once the index knows which printings come in foil, the app will not let you record a foil of one that was never printed that way.

When something goes wrong

A land will not scan

Basics need the artist line if the collector number will not read, and that needs an index downloaded since the app learned to keep artist names. Refresh the card index in Settings. The app says so explicitly rather than blaming the card.

A card scans as the wrong version

Open it in your collection and change the printing — the quantity, finish, condition and collection all travel with it.

Prices are zero, or missing

Prices only reach the index with a download. Use Update prices now in Settings, which fetches the cards you own and the cards in your decks in a couple of requests.

The phone cannot see the PC

Settings shows what every address did when asked, rather than one word. The usual causes: the desktop app is not running or phone scanning is off, Tailscale is not running on the phone, or the PC has moved to a different address since you paired.

A scan says it read the wrong thing

When the app cannot place a card it quotes what it actually read off it. That line is the useful thing to report — it is the difference between a bad photograph and a card the index does not have.
